# Roadmap

claude-referee compares what Claude Code says with the evidence, keeps a receipt of every decision and publishes its own error rate. It grows one measured step at a time: a feature moves into a release only with tests, and a check that changes Claude's behaviour only with a measurement. Each item below says what "done" means for it. Dates aren't promised; the order is. Until 1.0 every minor version is a theme and every patch is a fix.

Items marked **help wanted** are good places to start, and most need no TypeSafe key. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

What it is not: a model router, a context compactor or a general code reviewer. It doesn't replace Claude Code's `/verify` (which runs your app) or `/goal` (a Stop hook that reads only the conversation); it reads the evidence itself and records the decision. What was dropped or postponed, and why, is in [docs/decisions/dropped.md](docs/decisions/dropped.md).

## v0.1.x: hardening and live numbers

Released by 0.1.3: `done` v2 (runner output parsed in code), `verify` v2, the done-gate in `shadow` mode, the pack linter, `eval record` and `eval score`, `npm run ci:local`, and the live API checks and close-call option-order measurement ([results](docs/measurements.md)).

Released in 0.1.4:

- A request aborted after the API's headers arrived no longer kills the process with an uncaught error (SDK 0.6.0 bug; covered by a test that runs in CI on Node 22 and 24).
- The TypeSafe key goes only to `https://api.typesafe.ai`; another `TYPESAFE_BASE_URL` needs `REFEREE_BASE_URL_KEY`.
- `done` answers `missing` without asking Jev when the evidence has a non-zero exit code.
- The PHPUnit `Tests:` line is no longer read as a jest summary; `judge` and `decide` no longer let float arithmetic move a value that sits exactly on a band.
- CI scans the whole git history with a pinned gitleaks.
- A weekly job compares TypeSafe's `models.md`, `api.md` and `llms.txt` with recorded hashes (the rate limits changed once without an announcement we could find).

Released in 0.1.5 and 0.1.6: labels appended instead of rewriting `stops.jsonl`, `met` capped at `unsure` for skipped tests and for logs that say no tests ran, more runner parsers, the `claims` name, hold-outs 2 to 6 (see the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md)).

Still open:

- Trusted publishing with a manually triggered workflow behind a protected environment. Publishing itself is done (0.2.0 to 0.2.7, by the maintainer with a security-key login). No workflow publishes on a tag by itself.
- Whether `decide` should send both orders in one request. A pilot on the 39 close-call decisions on 2026-10-01 passed its registered rules (leaders within re-ask noise, 33% fewer input tokens) but showed no latency gain, and the saving is about $0.000015 per decision, so no code change is planned until a fresh hold-out says otherwise. Raw data and scripts: [jev-evals/experiments](jev-evals/experiments/README.md).

## v0.2: evidence in code, and thresholds in the open

- **Recorded-answer evals for every command.** Cases live in `jev-evals/<suite>/cases.jsonl` with separate dev and hold-out splits; `eval record` saves Jev's answers once and `eval score` gives the same result every time. `eval` handles `done`, `verify`, `judge` and `decide` suites and `--ablation context|reversed`; a decide suite is scored by leader agreement, not precision. `eval record` has `--max-requests` and `--max-usd` caps. Still to do: more labelled decide cases, ideally written by someone other than the repo author. **help wanted:** labelled cases, invented or public.
- **`done` v2, the rest.** A second hold-out of 45 cases was run and failed its registered check with one wrong `met` (a linter warning under "lint is clean"; [result](docs/measurements.md#done-v2-on-a-second-hold-out)). Two parsers (Python `unittest`, `cargo clippy` with warning counts) were added after it, and a third hold-out (46 cases) then failed on all three counts ([result](docs/measurements.md#done-v2-on-a-third-hold-out)). Afterwards skipped, risky or incomplete tests were capped at `unsure` in code and six parsers were added (`node --test` TAP, `eslint` summary with warnings, `tsc -b`, `golangci-lint`, `vite build`, `cargo build/check`). A fourth hold-out then failed too ([result](docs/measurements.md#done-v2-on-a-fourth-hold-out)); `met` is now also capped at `unsure` when the log says no tests ran. A fifth hold-out (47 cases, one labeller) then showed no wrong `met` and `missing` found in 0.92, but `met` found in only 0.50 of expected cases, so it failed the registered check on that count alone ([result](docs/measurements.md#done-v2-on-a-fifth-hold-out)); nothing was tuned on it. Eleven more parsers (`cargo nextest`, `prove`, `dart test`, `flutter test`, Julia, `behave`, `kaocha`, `tox`, `biome`, `next build`, `nix build`) were then written from real runs of the tools, and `met` is capped at `unsure` for cut-off, empty, cancelled or flaky parsed runs and for a lint criterion with parsed warnings; the 12 cases they changed moved to dev (9 of 11 expected `met` now `met`; dev numbers, not a pass; [sources](docs/decisions/runner-parsers-met-recall.md)). A sixth hold-out (53 cases in tools without parsers, a separate second labeller, plus a separate 8-case group for the new parsers) failed on all three counts: 2 wrong `met` (exit-code-only lint logs), `missing` found 0.84, `met` found 0.50, with `all tests pass` found in 1 of 11 expected `met` and lint or build criteria in 10 of 11; the parser group had 0 wrong `met` and 4 of 5 expected `met` found, a successful `next build` was missed ([result](docs/measurements.md#done-v2-on-a-sixth-hold-out)); nothing was tuned on it. The exit-code-only lint cap was then widened to failure and skip wording and swallowed exit codes (Jev p 0.96 on the second round, both orders; replayed after the rule it catches both sixth-hold-out wrong `met` and 3 true ones; fitted to those cases, not a clean test; [record](docs/decisions/exit-code-only-lint-wide.md)). Six invented hold-outs have not passed, and every one finds new tools without a parser. `done` v2 stays "not measured". Whether an exit code alone may give `met` is closed ([record](docs/decisions/exit-code-only-met.md)): yes, except that a lint or clean criterion with a warning or notice in the log is capped at `unsure` (Jev p 0.91, both orders; fitted to one case, so not a clean test). Also: parsers checked against real runs of each tool (no wrong `met`, `missing` recall of 0.9 or better, less input than v1). **help wanted:** real output samples from pytest, `go test`, PHPUnit, RSpec, `dotnet test`, Mocha, ESLint, Ruff, Vitest, Jest, Cargo and tsc, in the formats your CI prints.
- **Injection defence.** `done` sends only parsed facts when a runner is recognised and can never return `met` for unparsed text, and the injection suite plus the adversarial groups of four invented hold-outs (a note to the reviewer at the start, middle or end of a log; an authority claim as the only evidence; a test named "all tests passed"; forged summaries) produced no wrong `met` from an injected line, so a detector question for `done` is not planned. `claims` already has one. The surfaces still open are the Stop done-gate (task and final message are free text) and `judge` items.
- **`claims`.** `verify` v2 is now also `claims` (`verify` stays as an alias until 1.0, so it isn't confused with Claude Code's `/verify`; done in main). Open: whether the number and backtick checks or the injection gate can be loosened without a wrong `supported`; each needs a registered design first. A Turkish claim set (44 cases) held: no wrong `supported`, 0.76 of true claims confirmed against 0.79 in English ([result](docs/measurements.md#claims-on-turkish-sources)).
- **More redaction patterns.** Done for the formats in [redaction-patterns.md](docs/decisions/redaction-patterns.md); **help wanted:** formats seen in the wild that have a distinctive prefix, each with stop and keep fixtures.
- **`decide` policy.** Settled: two requests for every option count ([decide-policy.md](docs/decisions/decide-policy.md)). Reopen with a measurement of 3, 5 or 6 options or a lower-noise K3 re-run. That measurement now exists ([decide-order-scale.md](docs/decisions/decide-order-scale.md)): it supports a balanced 2n design over two orders on near-ties only. Done in main on 2026-10-06 for two-order ties, after a registered replay and a live check ([decide-balanced-near-ties.md](docs/decisions/decide-balanced-near-ties.md)); chosen with Jev below the bar and recorded as such ([next-work-2026-10-06.md](docs/decisions/next-work-2026-10-06.md)).

## v0.3: the done-gate, measured on real stops

- **`soft` mode** that leaves a note in the transcript without an error, next to `off`, `shadow` and `active` (done, in tag `v0.2.0` and later: a `systemMessage` warning only).
- **Labelling.** `receipts --stops` already lists stops and `--label <id> --right|--wrong` marks them. A weak `right` hint from the user's next message (reported breakage or a repeated request; approval or silence suggests nothing, so it can't fake a `wrong`) and a `claims_done` threshold suggestion with exact Clopper-Pearson intervals, produced only with at least 10 human labels per class, are done in main; the suggestion can only raise the threshold.
- **A base-rate study.** Shadow mode on two real projects for two weeks, one of them public. Kill criterion: if fewer than 2 of at least 100 stops are really a wrong "done", `active` is not recommended and the gate stays in `soft`. With no real stops to label, a self-generated version is registered in [session-base-rate.md](docs/decisions/session-base-rate.md) and its harness is in `scripts/session-study/` (24 seeded tasks, a hidden verifier per task, a budget cap); it measures this task mix only, not real projects. **Run on 2026-10-01 (119 sessions, 100 asked stops): 1 wrong "done", so the kill criterion fires and `active` is not recommended; the gate stays in `soft`.** The gate blocked all 100 asked stops, 99 of them on correct work, so on this mix it detects "claimed success without a counted passing check", not wrong "done"; the one wrong "done" was blocked, the weak hint could not fire on single-prompt sessions, and no threshold suggestion was possible (1 right label against 99 wrong). Real-project shadow stops are now recorded but none is labelled, so the base rate there is unmeasured ([result](docs/measurements.md#the-stop-gate-on-self-generated-sessions-base-rate-study)).
- **Real shadow data, unlabelled.** Real stops are recorded in `shadow` on the maintainer's machine (128 in the replay of [stop-check-detector-tools-result.md](docs/decisions/stop-check-detector-tools-result.md#real-use-replay)). Since 5f6d271 a stop skipped while background tasks run also records the turn's counts, marks, `turn`, `bg_pending` and `would_ask`: data only, it still asks nothing, and no policy for asking on such stops is registered. Since 3081434 the check detector counts more real toolchain checks (`node --test`, `php -l`, `phpstan`, `composer` scripts, `oxlint`, `ci` and `verify` scripts and others), fitted to tools seen in real use and replayed on the recorded stop suites with no asked wrong done moving to skipped. Labels are the missing piece for a real base rate.
- **State design study.** The gate's state carries no change content, so Jev cannot tell a wrong done from a true one (`claims_verified` AUC 0.3 to 0.4 in every variant that keeps the existing questions, even with edit contents, parsed check output and the README added). A new question about the task's requirements, with edit contents capped at 2 000 characters, separates them on a task-split hold-out (AUC 0.867) but misses 3 of 9 wrong dones at the dev threshold and is weaker within hard tasks (0.643); registered in [stop-state-design.md](docs/decisions/stop-state-design.md). Not built: sending edit contents to TypeSafe would be a privacy change and would have to be opt-in. Open: more wrong dones per task type to narrow the intervals.
- **Requirements question without contents.** The requirements-style question that separated wrong dones with edit contents (AUC 0.867) does not separate on the state the gate sends, with or without parsed check counts and line counts (fresh AUC 0.626, interval 0.432 to 0.791); registered in [stop-requirements-question.md](docs/decisions/stop-requirements-question.md). Nothing built. The state changes that remain are the ones that carry the code or the requirements text, both privacy changes that would have to be opt-in.
- **Recorded-session evals**, done in main with the repo's own `eval` instead of `claude plugin eval`: that runner cannot replay a recorded final message or run a verifier, so it stays an optional wiring check outside the gates. A suite with command `stop` replays redacted transcripts through the hook's analysis, skip rule, request and decision; 12 synthetic seed cases are recorded in `jev-evals/stop-sessions` ([measurements](docs/measurements.md)), and `jev-evals/stop-hard` holds 23 redacted hard-study sessions (all 15 wrong dones must stay blocked except the one the code skips by design; allowances 5 and 1 only go down), `jev-evals/stop-study` holds 13 redacted real study sessions (1 wrong "done" that must stay blocked, 5 skipped, 7 correct ones the gate blocks; the false-block allowance of 7 only goes down; `max_wrong_negative` is a new optional suite key). `scripts/session-study/cli.mjs fixture` turns more study sessions into cases.
- **A recipe for the project `verify` skill.** Since Claude Code 2.1.286 Claude runs a project or user skill named `verify` before committing; the recipe is one line telling it to pipe the test output to `claude-referee done`. The plugin doesn't write that file itself. Done in main: [docs/verify-skill.md](docs/verify-skill.md) and [docs/recipes/verify/SKILL.md](docs/recipes/verify/SKILL.md); the 2.1.286 behaviour is verified against the Claude Code changelog, and the doc notes that a project `verify` skill replaces the bundled `/verify` and that the run is prompt guidance, not a gate, and that test output is sent to the API. The recipe uses the exit-code form and covers the no-verdict case (npx could not run the package, a missing key, a crash).
- **Blind spots found by replaying real turns** (an outside team replayed 73 turns and most blocked sentences were true), split by evidence type:
  - Code side, done in main: a subagent or background-task finish notification no longer starts a new turn (it used to hide the edits before it), subagent launches and finish reports are counted, a pass from the previous turn is marked `stale_pass`, and a truncated Bash output (saved-to-file preview, a `[N lines truncated]` gap, a command moved to the background) can never count as a passing check. Marker shapes come from real transcripts (`test/stopgate-marks.test.ts`). The marks are recorded in `stops.jsonl` and not sent to Jev: sending them needs a measurement first.
  - Jev side, measured and left unchanged: negative sentences ("I haven't committed"). On 40 invented messages (two labellers, 40 of 40 agreement) the current `stop.*` wording blocked none of the 20 negated ones and 19 of 20 true done-claims on dev plus hold-out; two negated messages came near the `claims_done` threshold, so the wording stays and the registered screen ended the test there ([record](docs/decisions/stop-negation.md)). The replay's finding is not reproduced by invented messages; settling it needs labelled real stops.
- **Stats that count failures.** `receipts --stops` reports `error_rate` and `p95_all_ms` next to the answered-calls figures (done); the gate numbers use them.

- **Hard-task study result (2026-10-02, 76 sessions, 72 asked stops, 7.88 USD, [measurements](docs/measurements.md#the-stop-gate-on-hard-tasks-wrong-done-study)).** 15 wrong dones in 74 usable sessions (0.203, 0.118 to 0.312; sonnet alone 0.093), recall 14 of 15, precision 14 of 65 (0.215), false blocks 51 of 53 true dones asked, `claims_verified` AUC 0.398 (no separation), p95 631 ms, no Jev errors.
